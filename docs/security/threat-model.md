# Threat model

flust helps people quit pornography. Its data reveals intimate behavior: exposure is a privacy harm in itself, and can lead to shame, discrimination or blackmail. The design goal is that this data never leaves the device and is unreadable without unlocking it.

## Sensitive data

- Journal entries.
- Streak and lapse history, with where, when and in what context each lapse happened.
- Emergency screen visits: when they started and ended, and how.
- Blocking configuration (whether the filter and Strict YouTube are on), from the content filter of V0.2. On Android it lives outside the database, encrypted under its own key, so the filter can start at boot. A pending request to weaken the filter lives in the encrypted database ([0040](../decisions/0040-filter-delay.md)).
- The DNS lookups that pass through the Android filter, which reveal the sites visited. They are neither logged nor cached.
- The error report: no user content, but the times of errors show when the app was used.

## Threats and mitigations

| Threat                                                                  | Mitigation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Data sent to a server, an analytics service or a third-party SDK        | No backend, no accounts, no ads, no analytics, no crash reporter. Network calls and data-collecting SDKs are forbidden (AGENTS.md). In V0.1, production Android builds had no `INTERNET` permission, so the app could not open a socket itself, even through a dependency. From V0.2 they request it for the content filter ([0041](../decisions/0041-filter-network-permissions.md)); the release check still fails on a build that requests a blocked or unexpected permission. In flust's own code, a test and lint rules fail on the network APIs and on URLs outside the links handed to other apps ([0030](../decisions/0030-production-android-permissions.md)). |
| Someone with access to the device or its file system reads the data     | Data is encrypted at rest with keys held in Keychain / Android Keystore.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Data extracted from a device backup                                     | The database is encrypted with SQLCipher. Its key is stored with `AFTER_FIRST_UNLOCK_THIS_DEVICE_ONLY` and is never backed up or synced. Android backup and device-to-device transfer are disabled by `allowBackup="false"` and data extraction rules that exclude everything.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| A secret, key or token committed to the repository                      | `.gitignore` excludes key and env files. gitleaks scans staged changes in the pre-commit hook.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| A compromised or malicious dependency                                   | New dependencies must be justified and minimal. Dependency build scripts are denied by default (`pnpm-workspace.yaml`). Dependabot waits 7 days before proposing a release. CI installs from the lockfile only.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Someone holding the unlocked phone opens flust                          | An optional lock asks for the device's biometrics or passcode, after a chosen delay in the background, with a growing wait between attempts. The time away and the wait are measured on a clock that keeps running while the phone sleeps and that changing the date does not move. The content is covered as soon as the app leaves the foreground. A notification tap waits for the unlock. See [0023](../decisions/0023-app-lock.md) and [0024](../decisions/0024-lock-wait.md).                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| A notification reveals what flust is about                              | Texts are neutral and have no title. They may show on the lock screen, depending on the device's settings: on Android when sensitive content is shown there, the default on most devices, and on iOS when previews are shown. The reminders screen explains how to hide previews on iOS.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| A screenshot or the app switcher shows the content                      | In production builds, screenshots and screen recording are blocked and the recents preview is hidden on Android (`FLAG_SECURE`, set by `modules/flust-privacy` as the activity is created, before the first frame), and the app switcher preview is blurred on iOS. A protection that cannot be applied is recorded in the local error log ([0025](../decisions/0025-screen-privacy.md)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| A push service learns that the device runs flust                        | Notifications are local. iOS has no push entitlement; Android links no Firebase or Google Play services code ([0021](../decisions/0021-local-notifications.md)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| A notification tap reaches an unexpected screen                         | A tap carries only a route, checked against a fixed list (home, check-in). Android receivers are not exported.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| A web page detects that flust is installed, or opens one of its screens | Production builds register no URL scheme: no intent filter opens one on Android, and the `Info.plist` has no URL type on iOS. The Android release check fails on a build that has one. A link that still reaches the app opens home at launch and is ignored while it runs ([0035](../decisions/0035-no-url-scheme-in-production.md)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| The keyboard learns what is typed in flust and suggests it elsewhere    | On Android, every text field asks the keyboard not to learn its content (`IME_FLAG_NO_PERSONALIZED_LEARNING`). Keyboards may ignore the request; Gboard honors it. iOS has no such API: see the known limitations ([0036](../decisions/0036-keyboard-learning.md)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| An error report carries personal data                                   | Entries keep only fields matching strict patterns (time, kind, route path, error class, flust error code, stack frames as function, file, line and column), never messages. The log is encrypted with AES-GCM under its own keystore key. It leaves only through the share sheet, after the person has seen the exact text. In safe mode, which sits outside the app lock, the report first asks for the device's authentication, unless a read confirms the device has no screen lock ([0028](../decisions/0028-error-boundaries-and-safe-mode.md), [0029](../decisions/0029-local-error-log.md)).                                                                     |
| A compromised CI workflow                                               | Workflows run with `contents: read`, do not persist checkout credentials and pin actions by commit SHA.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

## Known limitations

- On iOS, the encrypted database file is included in iCloud and computer backups: expo-file-system cannot set `isExcludedFromBackup` without custom native code. The backup holds ciphertext only, since the key is never backed up. A backup restored to another phone cannot be decrypted, and the app offers a reset. To check with iOS testing, and to fix with a native module if needed.
- The app lock is a gate in the interface, not a key: the data key is not bound to biometrics, so a lock bypass on a rooted or jailbroken device reads the data. This keeps data readable after biometric changes. Someone who knows the device passcode can open flust.
- The launch counter (`launch-attempts`), which opens safe mode after repeated failed launches, is a plain file holding one number ([0028](../decisions/0028-error-boundaries-and-safe-mode.md)). Someone holding the phone can reach safe mode by closing flust twice during the splash screen; it shows no data, the report asks for the device's authentication, and erasing everything needs two confirmations, like uninstalling the app.
- iOS has no API that asks the system keyboard not to learn what is typed, so notes, check-in notes and custom reasons and plans may feed its suggestions. flust does not work around it. On Android, the request not to learn is only a request: a keyboard may ignore it ([0036](../decisions/0036-keyboard-learning.md)).
- The planned reminders are stored outside the encrypted database, in Android's no-backup storage and in iOS's notification center: their times and neutral texts only.
- In development builds only, expo-sqlite registers open databases with the Expo dev tools, which talk to the local development server. Release builds do not.
- From V0.2, the Android content filter forwards allowed DNS lookups, so the production build requests `INTERNET`, and `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE` to see strict Private DNS and another VPN. Network code is confined to the filter's Kotlin module, sockets go only to the resolvers of its chain, Quad9, Cloudflare and DNS4EU, over DNS over TLS, checked against the system certificates only, the VPN service never touches the database or its key, and no query is logged or cached. The permission also lets a dependency open sockets, which the source scan does not cover: dependencies rely on review. The release check fails on a build without the filter's VPN service or one of its permissions, the two network ones and the two foreground service ones ([0047](../decisions/0047-filter-foreground-service.md)), or with any other VPN service, or with classes of the development modules ([0044](../decisions/0044-production-modules.md)). An independent audit comes before the public release ([0033](../decisions/0033-android-filter-network-access.md), [0041](../decisions/0041-filter-network-permissions.md)).
- The resolver that answers sees the names that the filter allows, with the phone's IP address, as any resolver would: Quad9, or Cloudflare then DNS4EU when Quad9 does not answer, as in France from 24 November 2026. Quad9's policy is not to log client addresses. Cloudflare deletes its logs and the truncated addresses within 25 hours, keeps aggregates such as the list of names asked, and lets APNIC research its anonymised logs. DNS4EU keeps query logs with a pseudonym of the address for six months, for threat research with its partners, and says queries are not sold, profiled or used for advertising. DNS4EU applies court-ordered blocks. flust cannot verify any of them ([0045](../decisions/0045-resolver-chain.md), [Content filter](../product/content-filter.md#resolver)).
- The filter stops the impulse, not a determined adult: strict Private DNS, another VPN, turning the VPN off in the system settings, Clear data, a second phone or uninstalling flust get around it. The 20-minute delay only applies inside flust. flust shows when the filter is not protecting ([Content filter](../product/content-filter.md#known-limits)).
- iOS has no permission to remove for network access, and no check runs on the iOS build. The source scan and the lint rules catch network APIs in flust's own code only; dependencies and their native code rely on review.

## Out of scope

- A device that is unlocked and in someone else's hands, beyond what the optional lock covers.
- A compromised operating system.
